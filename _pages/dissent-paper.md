How does dissent feature in a central bank with fully transparent deliberations? By studying a uniquely transparent central bank, the Swedish Riksbank, I study in detail the causes and consequences of dissent in central bank communication. First, I map dissent to governor demographics and highlight some characteristics co-varying strongly with general dissent, for example, having a higher academic degree. Second, I show that a one-SD increase in general dissent reduces short term interest rate effects by roughly a third compared to the mean. Furthermore, studying the effects of the direction of dissent, I observe that dissenting in the dovish direction drives both shorter and longer rates upwards. Finally, when studying impulse responses from monetary policy shocks induced by transcript releases in different dissent regimes, I see evidence that dissent has an effect on the shape of the subsequent interest rate path.
